## 📝 Summary

We explored the foundations of automated negotiation in multi-agent systems, beginning with how autonomous agents with different preferences and goals reach agreements through offers and counteroffers. We examined the basic negotiation model, including negotiation issues, outcome spaces, utility functions, protocols, and strategies. We discussed the differences between single-issue and multi-issue negotiation, the role of trade-offs in reaching mutually beneficial agreements, and common concession strategies such as Boulware, Conceder, and Linear. We also introduced the Automated Negotiating Agents Competition (ANAC) and the Automated Negotiation League (ANL), focusing on how negotiating agents are designed and evaluated.

Following the introductory slides, we took a deeper look at two research papers. The first, _A Heuristic Personality-Based Bilateral Multi-Issue Bargaining Model in Electronic Commerce_, explored how the OCEAN personality model can be incorporated into automated bargaining through risk and cooperation factors, fuzzy inference, and personality-dependent decision-making. The second, _POPPONENT: Highly Accurate, Individually and Socially Efficient Opponent Preference Model in Bilateral Multi Issue Negotiations_, introduced a perceptron-based approach to learning an opponent's preferences from exchanged offers using multi-bipartite incremental gradient descent. Together, these papers provided two perspectives on designing negotiating agents: modeling agents' behavioral characteristics and learning about their opponents.
